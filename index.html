How the maths works

The complete calculation is implemented in approximately 300 lines of client-side JavaScript and runs entirely within the browser; no server-side processing occurs. Each modelling decision below is linked to the peer-reviewed source it is based on, and simplifications are noted explicitly where the underlying evidence warrants it. View the source.

Nothing here is a black box. Every step below runs right in your browser, in about 300 lines of readable JavaScript, and every decision links to the research it's based on. Where we've simplified something, we say so. See the code for yourself.

1

Resting metabolism (BMR)

How many calories your body burns at rest

BMR = 10·weightkg + 6.25·heightcm − 5·age + s

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation1 was identified as the most accurate of the commonly used predictive equations in a systematic review of validation studies against measured resting energy expenditure2. The constant s takes a value of +5 for a testosterone-dominant metabolic profile and −161 for an estrogen-dominant profile, a distinction driven by hormone-dependent body composition rather than gender identity. Because gender-affirming hormone therapy produces measurable shifts in lean mass and fat distribution over the initial months of treatment14,15, the constant s is interpolated linearly over the first 6 months of therapy rather than assigned as a fixed binary value.

Maintenance energy expenditure (TDEE)

How many calories you burn in a normal day

TDEE = BMR × PAL (1.2 – 1.9)

Physical activity level multipliers follow the FAO/WHO/UNU joint expert consultation on human energy requirements3, ranging from 1.2 for a sedentary lifestyle to 1.9 for an athlete engaged in daily intensive training.

Activity multipliers come from the FAO/WHO/UNU report on human energy needs3, ranging from 1.2 for sedentary to 1.9 for an athlete training hard every day.

3

Energy deficit

The deficit: how big a gap to create

deficit = pace%BW/wk × weight × 7700 ÷ 7 capped at 25% of TDEE

Slower rates of weight loss are associated with better preservation of lean mass: elite athletes losing approximately 0.7 percent of body weight per week maintained, and in some cases gained, lean mass, while those losing approximately 1.4 percent per week experienced lean mass loss6. This underlies both the available pace options (0.5 to 1 percent per week) and the 25 percent deficit cap8. The figure of approximately 7,700 kcal per kilogram of fat tissue derives from Wishnofsky's classic analysis4. It functions as a useful planning constant, though the true energy cost per kilogram lost shifts as body composition changes over the course of a diet5; the projected timeline should be read as an estimate rather than a guarantee. This deficit produces a suggested target only; actual daily intake is calculated live from the three macro sliders below, and the results screen compares the two directly.

4

Protein: preservation of lean mass

Protein: your muscle insurance

protein = 1.0 – 3.0 g × reference weightkg default 2.0

A meta-analysis of 49 trials found that the benefit of protein intake for lean mass gain plateaus at approximately 1.6 g/kg/day, with the confidence interval extending to approximately 2.27, the basis for the optimal range identified above. Dieting athletes, particularly those already lean, may benefit from intakes at or above the upper end of this range8,9. Protein is also the most satiating macronutrient10 and has the highest thermic effect of food, with 20 to 30 percent of its energy expended during digestion11. Above a BMI of 30, protein is dosed against target weight rather than current weight, a practical approximation for the fat-free-mass-based dosing used in the primary literature8.

A meta-analysis of 49 trials found that protein's benefit for muscle levels off at around 1.6 g/kg a day, with the upper end of the confidence range reaching about 2.27, which is why that's our sweet spot. Dieting athletes, especially lean ones, may benefit from going even higher8,9. Protein is also the most filling macro10 and burns the most calories just to digest, 20 to 30% of its own energy11. 5

Fat: endocrine considerations

Fat: keeping your hormones running

fat = 0.4 – 1.6 g × reference weightkg default 0.8 · warning below 0.6

A meta-analysis found that low-fat diets reduce testosterone levels in men12, and contest-preparation reviews recommend maintaining fat at approximately 15 to 30 percent of calories even during aggressive deficits8. Dietary fat additionally serves as the transport medium for the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K.

A meta-analysis found that low-fat diets lower testosterone in men12, and contest-prep guides recommend keeping fat at roughly 15 to 30% of calories even in a deep cut8. Fat also carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K around your body.

Carbohydrate: the third independent lever

Carbs: the third lever

calories = protein×4 + carbs×4 + fat×9 derived, not fixed

Protein, fat, and carbohydrate are each set independently, and total daily energy intake is the sum of the three, calculated live rather than fixed in advance. This makes the trade-off explicit: total calories can be lowered only by reducing one or more of the three macronutrients, and the results screen reports how far the current combination sits from the suggested pace-based target. The carbohydrate zone thresholds reflect sports-nutrition guidance on carbohydrate requirements scaling with training load13.

Protein, fat, and carbs are each their own dial now, and your total calories for the day are just the three of them added up, calculated live instead of fixed in advance. That makes the trade-off obvious: the only way to bring your total down is to bring one of the three sliders down, and the results screen tells you exactly how far you are from the suggested target. The carb zone labels follow sports-nutrition guidance on carb needs scaling with how much you train13.

Open science, open source

  • Auditable. The complete source code is MIT-licensed and publicly available; every constant referenced above appears verbatim in the implementation.
  • Private. No server-side processing, analytics, or account system is used. Input data remains on-device, stored only in browser local storage.
  • Bounded in scope. These are population-level equations, and individual variance is real: a margin of error of approximately 10 percent on BMR estimates is typical2. Monitoring actual weight trend over 2 to 3 weeks and adjusting accordingly is recommended.
  • Auditable: the full source is MIT-licensed and public. Every number above appears exactly as written in the code.
  • Private: no server, no analytics, no account. Your inputs never leave your device, they're only saved in your browser's local storage.
  • Honest about limits: these are population-level equations, and real people vary. About 10% error on BMR estimates is normal2. Track your actual weight trend for 2 to 3 weeks and adjust from there.
