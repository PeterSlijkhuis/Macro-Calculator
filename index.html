How the maths works

Nothing here is a black box. Every step below runs in your browser, in ~300 lines of readable JavaScript, and every modelling decision links to the peer-reviewed work it's based on. Where the evidence is a simplification, we say so.

1

Resting metabolism

BMR = 10·weightkg + 6.25·heightcm − 5·age + s

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation1, the most accurate of the common predictive equations in a systematic review of validation studies2. The constant s is +5 for a testosterone-dominant metabolism and −161 for an estrogen-dominant one — a difference driven by hormone-dependent body composition, not identity. Because gender-affirming hormone therapy measurably shifts lean mass and fat distribution over the first months of treatment14,15, we interpolate s linearly over the first 6 months on HRT rather than forcing a binary choice.

2

Maintenance calories

TDEE = BMR × PAL (1.2 – 1.9)

Physical activity level multipliers follow the FAO/WHO/UNU human energy requirements framework3, from sedentary (1.2) to athlete (1.9).

3

The deficit

deficit = pace%BW/wk × weight × 7700 ÷ 7 capped at 25% of TDEE

Slower cuts protect muscle: athletes losing ~0.7% of body weight per week kept (and even gained) lean mass, while ~1.4%/week lost it6 — hence the 0.5–1% pace options and the hard 25% cap8. The ≈7,700 kcal per kg of fat tissue figure comes from Wishnofsky's classic analysis4; it's a useful planning constant, though real-world loss per kcal shifts as body composition changes5, so treat the timeline as an estimate, not a promise.

4

Protein — the muscle insurance

protein = 1.0 – 3.0 g × reference weightkg default 2.0

A meta-analysis of 49 trials found benefits of protein for lean mass plateau around ~1.6 g/kg/day, with the confidence interval reaching ~2.27 — that's our "sweet spot". Dieting athletes may benefit from the higher end and beyond, especially when lean8,9. Protein is also the most satiating macronutrient10 and has the highest thermic effect — 20–30% of its calories are spent digesting it11. Above a BMI of 30 we dose protein on your target weight instead of current weight, a practical stand-in for the fat-free-mass dosing used in the literature8.

5

Fat — the hormone floor

fat = 0.4 – 1.6 g × reference weightkg default 0.8 · warning below 0.6

Meta-analysis shows low-fat diets reduce testosterone levels in men12, and contest-prep reviews recommend keeping fat at roughly 15–30% of calories even in deep cuts8. Dietary fat also carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K.

6

Carbs — the remainder, on purpose

carbs = (calories − protein×4 − fat×9) ÷ 4

Energy balance is a budget: once protein and fat are set, carbs are what's left — making that explicit is more honest than pretending three independent dials could all be free. The zone labels reflect sports-nutrition guidance on carbohydrate needs scaling with training load13.

Open science, open source

  • Auditable — the full source is MIT-licensed; every constant above appears verbatim in the code.
  • Private — no server, no analytics, no account. Your inputs never leave your device (they're saved in your browser's local storage only).
  • Honest about limits — these are population-level equations with real individual variance (±10% on BMR is common2). Track your actual weight trend for 2–3 weeks and adjust.
